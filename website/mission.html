Built out of frustration.

Why Murmint exists, who made it, and everything that happened along the way.

proimpacts Murmint TeamThe person behind Murmint

Our mission

Voice typing should be fast, light and fair. Murmint turns what you say into clean, formatted text in any Windows app. It hears you with its own free speech model on your PC, formats with the AI services you already have, and does it without a subscription and without eating your computer.

Where it started

It started with frustration. I talk faster than I type, so I tried the popular dictation apps, and every one of them left me angry. They charged high monthly prices for a small number of words, buried the one thing I wanted under features I never asked for, and used a ridiculous amount of my computer's resources just to sit in the background.

At the same time I was building my other project, AuraLink, with Claude helping me through front-end and UI problems. I kept hitting my usage limits, and every time I did, I was stuck typing long explanations by hand again.

The epiphany

Somewhere in the middle of that, it clicked. Everything these apps were charging me for was already within reach. The speech models and the language models were right there, on services I could use with my own keys, for fractions of a cent. What was missing was not more features. It was a small, fast tool that got out of the way: hold a key, talk, and get clean text where my cursor already was.

So I decided to stop paying for bloat and build it myself, the way I wanted it to work.

Building it

Murmint took five to six months of work. I did about 60% of the work and Claude about 40%: I drove the idea, the design and the decisions, and Claude worked alongside me on the code, including the big migration that moved the heart of the app into Rust.

That move is why Murmint stays so small and quick. One native engine, about 4.5 MB, does the work: the text rules, the model router, your keys, the shortcuts and the typing. The window you see is only the interface, a thin Electron shell that opens when you want it and is gone when you close it.

When I lost it

It was not a straight road. After all that time building, the GitHub account I had been working on was accidentally deleted, and most of the code went with it.

What I had left was the idea, the design, every decision I had made along the way, and the parts of the project I still had. I uploaded those to a new account and started putting the rest back together. The project survived, and so did the point of it: I still wanted this tool to exist, and I still couldn't find it anywhere.

It also explains something you might notice. The history of the new GitHub repository starts at that re-upload, not at the beginning. That isn't because Murmint is new. It's because the original account, with all of its history, is gone.

Starting again

Since then I have been rebuilding and improving it quickly. A lot has changed:

  • Much lighter. About 20 MB of memory while it waits in your tray, and the memory it isn't using goes back to Windows.
  • Murmint 1, its own speech model. It runs on your PC, so turning your voice into text is free, private and works offline. It downloads during setup and is the default for everyone.
  • Simple model choice. For speech and for formatting, pick Automatic (the best model you have), Cheapest, or one model yourself. Add a provider, tick the models you want, and Murmint tests how fast each one answers.
  • Easier setup. Sign in with Discord, Murmint 1 downloads, and you can dictate straight away. Add an AI service when you want your words formatted.
  • Any AI provider. OpenAI, Anthropic, Groq, Gemini, OpenRouter and many more, any OpenAI- or Claude-compatible server, or Ollama and LM Studio on your own PC.
  • A style for each app. Professional in Slack, casual in Discord, exact in your code editor, with your own notes and rules for each one.
  • More than dictation. Translate selected text in place, Better Clipboard on Win + V, clean up text you already typed with Ctrl + Alt + F, and Better prompts for AI apps.
  • A new look, a new installer and uninstaller, and updates that install themselves from murmint.app.
  • A community. A Discord server for the people testing it, where bugs and ideas go straight to me.

Where it's now

Murmint is in closed alpha. A small group of testers are using it and I'm fixing what they find. Later it opens up as an open alpha, so anyone can download it. A signed installer, so Windows stops warning about it, comes after that.

And honestly, it has also been a fun project. It's the tool I wanted to exist, built because nobody else had built it quite right.

What that means for you

  • No subscription. The app is free and speech is free with Murmint 1. For formatting you pay your AI provider directly, usually pennies, or nothing with a free tier or a local model.
  • Light. One small Rust engine does the work.
  • Yours. Your keys, history and vocabulary stay on your PC, audio is never stored, and the code is open under the GPL.
