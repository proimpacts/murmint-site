Chapter one

Ten Tired Fingers

Most of us didn't notice how much we typed. It crept up on us. A few emails in the morning, a few messages after lunch, a document that needed "just a couple of lines". Then one day we looked down at our hands at six in the evening and realised they had been busy since nine, and we couldn't remember most of what they'd written.

We were not short of words. That was never the problem. We could explain a plan to a colleague across the desk in thirty seconds. Ask us to write the same plan down, and it took twenty minutes, three drafts and a short walk. The words were all there. They were just stuck behind ten tired fingers.

Some of us tried to type faster. We learned shortcuts. We bought keyboards that sounded like rain on a tin roof. We got quicker, and we typed more, and we were exactly as tired as before.

What finally helped was simple, almost embarrassingly so. We stopped asking our fingers to carry everything. We held two keys, said what we meant the way we'd say it to a friend, and let go. The words came out tidy, finished, and in the right place.

Nobody here is against keyboards. We still use ours. We just don't make them do all the talking any more.
