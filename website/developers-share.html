I spent more time typing than building, so I built Murmint

A share from the developer of MurmintNovember 20265 minute read

Hi, I'm the developer of Murmint, and I'm a typer.

For most of the time I've been building software, I thought the hard part was the code. It wasn't. The hard part was everything around the code: the messages, the notes, the explanations. All of it went through my keyboard, one key at a time.

What a normal day looked like

A normal day went something like this:

  • Write some code.
  • Write a commit message explaining the code.
  • Reply to bug reports, asking for more detail, then explaining the fix.
  • Answer questions on Discord.
  • Write long prompts telling an AI exactly what I wanted, what not to touch, and why.
  • Update the docs, so the next person didn't have to ask.

By the evening I'd typed thousands of words, and most of them weren't code. The thinking took a few minutes. Getting it out through my fingers took the rest of the day.

Trying to talk instead

I knew I could say all of it much faster than I could type it, so I tried dictation. It didn't go well. The tools wrote down every "um". They put full stops in strange places. When I changed my mind halfway through a sentence, they kept both versions. I'd say "eight thirty, no wait, nine thirty" and get both times back, side by side, like a ransom note.

Fixing the text took longer than typing it would have. So I went back to typing, and I stayed annoyed about it.

I didn't want to stop typing because typing is bad. I wanted to stop typing the things I could just say.

So I built it

The idea was simple: hold two keys, say it the way it comes out, let go, and get back what I meant. Clean sentences, proper punctuation, paragraphs where they belong, and the "no wait" already sorted out. Right where my cursor was, in whatever app I had open.

The first version was rough. It worked, mostly, if you spoke slowly and didn't change your mind. Over time it got better at the things I actually do: emails, chat messages, lists, and prompts for AI tools, each written the way that kind of writing should look. I moved the engine to Rust so it stays light and out of the way in the background. I added Murmint 1, so speech can turn into text on your own PC. And I made the whole thing open source, because I wouldn't want a tool listening to me that I couldn't read.

There's no subscription. Speech on your PC is free, and if you want the AI clean-up, you plug in your own provider and pay them directly. For most people, that's pennies a month.

What changed

I still write code with a keyboard. But I don't type the paragraphs around it any more. Commit messages, Discord replies, bug reports and prompts all start with Ctrl + Win now. I talk, I let go, and I move on.

The strangest part is how much quieter my desk is. I hadn't noticed how much of my day was clatter until it stopped.

If this sounds like you

You don't have to give up your keyboard. You just don't have to type everything yourself. Try it on the next long reply you've been putting off, and see how it feels.
