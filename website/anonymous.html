Hello, my name isDanaWrites 60 emails a day

Forty minutes, one email

I've been coming for about three months. Before that I sent sixty emails a day, and every single one started with "Hope this finds you well." I never once wondered how it found them. I was just typing.

The worst day was a Tuesday. I wrote a reply to Maria, deleted it, wrote it again word for word, then sat there wondering if "Thanks!" was too much. Forty minutes. One email.

Now I say it the way I'd say it to her face, and I go and make a coffee. I've had the same mug for three months. I'd never noticed it before.

Hello, my name isLeo
Changes his mind a lot

Four messages to move one meeting

I change my mind a lot. Ask anyone. Ask my team. They've muted me.

I used to type "let's meet at 8:30", send it, then "actually 9:30", then "sorry, does 9:30 work?", then "ignore that, 9 is fine". Four messages to move one meeting. People kept replying to the wrong one.

The first time I said it out loud, I said "eight thirty, no wait, nine thirty", and only nine thirty showed up. One message. Nobody had to scroll.

Hello, my name isRavi
Backend engineer

One-word pull requests

For years my pull request descriptions were one word long. Usually "fix". On a good day, "fixes".

It wasn't that I didn't know what I'd changed. I knew exactly. I just couldn't face typing it all out after typing the code itself.

Now I say it on the way to get coffee: what changed, why, and what to test. My reviewers have started leaving comments like "thanks for the context". I didn't know people said that.

Hello, my name isJune
Novelist

Lists with one finger

I'm a novelist, so you'd think words would be my friends. They were, until I had to type all of them.

I'm also a list person. I used to stand in the hallway with my laptop on one arm, typing "passport" with one finger.

These days I just ramble: "um, passport, the charger, snacks, oh, and a jacket." I once said eleven ums in one sentence, and none of them made it onto the list. I felt seen.

Hello, my name isKai
Vibecoder

Don't change the colours

I spend all day typing to an AI that types code for me. I know how that sounds.

My prompts used to be one long sentence with no full stops, and every time, it changed my colours. I'd type DON'T CHANGE THE COLOURS in capitals, and it still changed the colours.

Now I talk to it like a colleague, and the prompt comes out numbered, with the "don't" part on its own at the bottom. The colours haven't changed in six weeks.

Our story

The developer of Murmint

Hi, I'm the developer of Murmint, and I'm a typer.

Building software mostly means typing. Code all day, then commit messages, bug reports, replies on Discord, and long prompts explaining to an AI exactly what I wanted. Most days the typing took longer than the thinking. I'd have the whole idea in my head, then spend twenty minutes squeezing it out through my fingers.

I tried the dictation tools that were around. They wrote down every "um", put full stops in strange places, and when I said "eight thirty, no wait, nine thirty", they kept both. Fixing the text took longer than typing it would have.

So I built the thing I wished I had: hold two keys, say it the way it comes out, and get clean writing back, right where the cursor is. I called it Murmint. I still type. Just a lot less.

A newcomer asks

Do I have to give up my keyboard?

No. You'll still need it for Ctrl + Win. We're not monsters.

Do I have to share?

Only when you want to. Murmint listens while you hold the keys, and stops when you let go.

How long until I feel something?

About two minutes. Install it, sign in, hold the keys and talk. The text appears where your cursor is.

Can I come alone?

Yes. Speech can run entirely on your PC with Murmint 1, and your audio is never stored.

What does it cost?

There's no subscription. Speech on your PC is free, and formatting uses the AI provider you choose, usually pennies a month.

Is Typers Anonymous real?

The group isn't. The app is, it's open source, and it's in alpha on Windows 10 and 11.
